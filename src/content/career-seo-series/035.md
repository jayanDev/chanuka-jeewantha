# Stop Listing Job Duties: Write Achievement-Based CV Bullet Points Instead

Achievement-based CV bullets explain your contribution rather than merely repeating the job description. They help an employer understand what you did with your responsibilities.

Not every bullet needs a percentage. Specific scope, a completed improvement or a clear deliverable can provide useful evidence when reliable numerical results are unavailable.

## Begin with a real piece of work

Choose a task, project or problem you remember clearly. Write what the situation required, what you personally did and what happened next. Keep supporting details in private notes so you can discuss the example later.

Do not begin by searching for an impressive verb. The action becomes clearer once the underlying contribution is understood.

## Use action, context and outcome

Duty: “Responsible for monthly reporting.”

Illustrative improvement: “Prepared monthly branch-performance reports, checked inconsistent entries and summarised unresolved issues for the finance team.”

If there is a verified result, add it. If not, the improved version still identifies the output, activity and audience. Avoid attaching a made-up time-saving percentage simply to make the bullet look achievement-focused.

## Choose evidence appropriate to the role

A customer-service bullet may discuss resolving an escalation or improving a handover. An engineering bullet may describe a technical deliverable and validation. A manager's bullet may explain a decision and the team or budget scope involved.

The relevant evidence depends on what the next employer needs. A technically impressive achievement may deserve less space if it is unrelated to the target role.

## Keep ownership accurate

Use “supported,” “coordinated,” “developed” or “led” according to your actual contribution. If a result came from a team, explain the part you owned. Do not present a company-wide improvement as your individual achievement without justification.

Confidential results can be described at an appropriate level of detail. Do not disclose customer names or private business data just to make a bullet more specific.

## Edit for readability

Keep one main idea per bullet. Remove introductory phrases such as “duties included being responsible for.” Check that a reader unfamiliar with your company can understand the terminology.

Read your strongest bullets aloud and prepare a short explanation of each. A [CV review](/services/packages/cv-review) can help identify vague claims and missing context; [professional writing](/services/packages/cv-writing) can help reorganise a duty-heavy document.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does every bullet need a measurable result?

No. Accurate scope, a delivered output or a documented improvement can be useful evidence without a numerical claim.

### Can routine work be an achievement?

Yes, when you explain the contribution, complexity or improvement. Do not exaggerate routine work, but do not assume it has no value.
