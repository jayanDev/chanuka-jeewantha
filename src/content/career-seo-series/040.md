# 10 Things You Should Remove From Your CV Today

Removing weak content can improve a CV more than adding another page. Review these ten items and keep only information that serves the application.

Use judgment rather than deleting blindly. A detail that is irrelevant to one vacancy may be required or valuable for another.

## Remove vague or duplicated wording

1. **Generic objectives.** Replace statements about wanting a challenging opportunity with a relevant professional introduction.
2. **Unsupported adjectives.** “Exceptional” and “world-class” need evidence; often the evidence can speak more clearly on its own.
3. **Repeated duties.** Similar bullets across several roles can hide progression and consume space.
4. **Copied job descriptions.** Explain what you actually contributed, not only what the position required.

Read each paragraph and ask what new information it provides. If another section already makes the same point, combine or remove the repetition.

## Remove misleading or unnecessary detail

5. **Invented results.** Replace unverifiable percentages with accurate scope or a documented deliverable.
6. **Skills you cannot explain.** Relevant learning can be labelled honestly; unsupported expertise should not remain.
7. **Unnecessary sensitive information.** Review identity numbers, personal categories and other people's contact details.
8. **Obsolete detail that overwhelms recent work.** Older school activities or early duties may need less space as your career develops.

Do not erase facts in a way that creates a misleading history. The aim is selective detail, not concealment.

## Remove submission hazards

9. **Editing leftovers.** Delete comments, unresolved tracked changes, template instructions and highlighted reminders from the final file.
10. **Broken or irrelevant links.** Test portfolio and profile links, and remove destinations that are inaccessible or unrelated to the role.

A link is useful only when the reader can reach and understand the evidence. Check access permissions before using a cloud document as a portfolio example.

## Rebuild the space around evidence

Use the space you recover for a clearer summary, stronger achievements or a relevant project. Do not immediately refill it with generic skills or decorative graphics.

Open the exported file after editing and inspect every page. Removing content can leave awkward gaps or headings detached from their paragraphs. Ask a reader whether the revised version makes your professional direction easier to understand.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should I remove all older jobs?

No. Keep an accurate chronology and relevant experience, but reduce unnecessary detail in older entries when appropriate.

### Is every hobby irrelevant?

No. A relevant activity may demonstrate useful experience, especially early in your career. Avoid including hobbies merely to fill space.
