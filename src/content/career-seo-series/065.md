# HR CV Writing: How to Position Recruitment, L&D and HR Operations Experience

An HR CV should make your specialism and actual responsibilities clear. Recruitment, learning and development, and HR operations share a function but involve different evidence.

Choose the target role before deciding which activities deserve prominence. A broad “HR duties” description makes it difficult to assess your fit.

## Separate the main areas of work

For recruitment, explain the stages you handled, such as vacancy coordination, screening support or interview scheduling. For L&D, describe learning needs, programme coordination or evaluation. For operations, identify records, systems and employee-service processes.

Do not imply responsibility for an entire employee lifecycle if you supported only selected activities. Accurate scope helps the reader understand your level.

## Show process and contribution

An illustrative example might describe maintaining an interview tracker and coordinating schedule changes between candidates and hiring teams. Another might explain preparing training materials and collecting participant feedback.

Add verified outcomes where available. If you claim reduced hiring time, define the period and stages measured rather than using an unexplained percentage.

## Demonstrate judgment and discretion

HR work can involve sensitive employee and candidate information. Keep names, medical details, complaints and other confidential records out of the CV and portfolio.

You can describe handling sensitive processes without revealing the people involved. Do not use an employee dispute as a dramatic anecdote that compromises confidentiality.

## Present systems and qualifications accurately

Name HR systems through the work you performed in them. Distinguish data entry, reporting, configuration and implementation responsibility. List qualifications and professional memberships with their correct status.

Avoid claiming legal or policy expertise beyond your actual role. If you supported compliance processes, describe the support rather than implying authority you did not hold.

## Match the next opportunity

For a specialist role, bring relevant examples forward. For an HR generalist position, show the breadth you genuinely have while maintaining clear ownership boundaries.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should I include confidential employee cases?

Do not disclose identifying or sensitive details. Describe your responsibility and process at an appropriate, authorised level.

### Can recruitment coordination support an HR generalist application?

It can provide relevant evidence, but do not imply experience in payroll, employee relations or other areas you have not actually handled.
