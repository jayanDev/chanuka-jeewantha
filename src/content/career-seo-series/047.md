# How to Add University Projects to Your CV Like Real Professional Experience

University projects can demonstrate practical ability when they are described with the same clarity as work experience. They should still be labelled as academic projects.

The goal is not to disguise study as employment. It is to explain the problem, your role, the methods and the output so an employer can assess what you learned to do.

## Choose projects for relevance

Select projects that connect to the vacancy. A data-analysis project may deserve more space for an analyst role, while a scheduling or process-design project may suit operations work.

Do not automatically choose the project with the highest grade if another demonstrates more relevant capability. Keep a private list so you can tailor the selection later.

## Give each project a useful heading

Include a clear project name, academic context and date. If the title is abstract, add a short description that explains the subject. State whether the work was individual, group-based or ongoing.

For a group project, name your responsibility. “Developed the login interface and validation checks” is more precise than claiming you built the entire application.

## Explain the work in a few focused bullets

- Identify the problem or objective.
- Describe what you personally designed, analysed or implemented.
- Name relevant tools or methods you actually used.
- Explain the delivered output or verified finding.

An illustrative example is cleaning survey responses, documenting exclusions and preparing charts for a final presentation. The value is in the method and output, not in calling the assignment “industry-leading.”

## Be careful with results

A model's classroom evaluation is not necessarily a production result. A prototype is not a deployed commercial product. Label the environment and limitations so the reader understands what was demonstrated.

If you use a metric, explain its basis when asked. Do not include impressive numbers from another team's work or from the assignment brief as if they were your results.

## Add a link when it helps

Link to a repository, report or portfolio page with a concise explanation. Check permissions and remove private datasets, credentials and material you cannot share. A broken or unexplained link is less useful than a clear CV description.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should projects go under employment?

Use a clearly labelled Projects or Academic Projects section unless the work was genuinely part of employment or an internship.

### Can I include a project without a public link?

Yes. Explain the work clearly and discuss what can be shared if asked. Confidentiality and permissions take priority over portfolio visibility.
