# Accounting & Finance CV Writing: How to Show More Than Daily Responsibilities

A finance CV should explain the processes you worked on, the scope of your responsibility and the decisions or controls your work supported.

“Prepared accounts” and “handled finance” are too broad. Specific evidence helps distinguish transaction processing, reporting, analysis and management responsibility.

## Identify the relevant finance function

Choose the target role and prioritise matching work. Accounts payable, management reporting, audit support and financial planning involve different evidence even when they share some tools.

Use accurate terms for the activities you performed. Supporting an audit is not the same as issuing an audit opinion, and preparing data for a forecast is different from owning the forecasting process.

## Explain the workflow and contribution

Describe what you reconciled, checked, reported or analysed and who used the output. An illustrative example might explain reconciling supplier statements, investigating unmatched items and preparing exceptions for review.

That gives context without claiming an unsupported reduction in errors. If you have a verified improvement, state the period and basis clearly.

## Include systems and credentials carefully

Name ERP, spreadsheet or reporting tools you actually used, with the relevant tasks. Avoid a long list of systems encountered only briefly.

State professional qualification status precisely. Membership, completed examinations, ongoing study and expected completion are different claims. Use the relevant designation only when you are entitled to it.

## Protect sensitive information

Finance work can involve confidential figures, supplier details and internal control information. Describe achievements at an appropriate level and do not attach private reports as portfolio evidence.

When a metric cannot be shared, explain scope or a delivered improvement. Strong writing does not require exposing information your employer expects you to protect.

## Show readiness for the next level

If you want a more senior role, identify evidence of reviewing work, explaining findings, coordinating deadlines or improving a process. Do not rely solely on years of service to communicate progression.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should I include routine month-end work?

Yes, when relevant. Explain the specific activities, scope and your responsibility rather than listing “month-end” without context.

### Can I list a qualification I am studying for?

Yes. Clearly state the current stage and expected completion where appropriate, without implying the qualification has already been awarded.
