# How to Write a Professional Summary That Makes Recruiters Keep Reading

A professional summary should help the reader interpret your CV. It identifies your career direction and strongest relevant capabilities before the detailed evidence begins.

Avoid treating it as a place for ambitious adjectives. “Dynamic, hardworking and passionate” can describe almost anyone. Specific context makes the opening useful.

## Write the summary after the evidence

Draft your experience and projects first. Identify the themes they support, then write the opening around those themes. This prevents claiming expertise that the rest of the document does not demonstrate.

Decide which role the CV targets. If you are applying for both marketing and operations work, the same opening may not give either employer a clear reason to continue.

## Use four ingredients

- Your actual professional identity or career stage.
- Relevant experience or educational context.
- Two or three capabilities that matter to the target role.
- A credible indication of the work or value you can contribute.

You do not need a sentence for every ingredient. Keep the paragraph concise and avoid listing every skill you have ever used.

## Compare a vague and a specific opening

Vague: “A motivated individual seeking a challenging position in a reputed organisation where I can use my skills.”

Illustrative alternative: “Customer-service professional experienced in order coordination, complaint resolution and daily service reporting. Comfortable working with sales and operations teams to track unresolved requests and keep customers informed.”

The alternative supplies context without inventing revenue figures or claiming an unrealistic outcome. Adapt the structure to your real experience rather than copying the example as a personal claim.

## Handle a career change honestly

Name the transferable foundation and relevant preparation. A teacher moving toward learning and development might emphasise training design, learner assessment and recent workplace-learning study. They should not call themselves an experienced corporate L&D manager without that background.

## Remove claims that add little

Check each adjective and ask what evidence supports it. Replace broad claims with specific strengths where possible. Avoid “expert” if your experience is introductory, and do not add years of experience without checking the relevant timeline.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long should the summary be?

A few focused lines are a useful starting point. The goal is an accurate introduction, not a second cover letter.

### Can a graduate write a professional summary?

Yes. State your degree or career direction, relevant projects and demonstrated capabilities without pretending to have years of professional experience.
