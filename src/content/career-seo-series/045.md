# Undergraduate CV Guide: How to Turn University Experience Into Career Value

University experience becomes useful CV evidence when you explain the work behind it. A degree title alone does not show how you analyse, organise, build or communicate.

Choose evidence relevant to the role you want. The aim is to make your preparation understandable without presenting academic work as professional employment.

## Map activities to capabilities

List projects, presentations, research, society work and practical placements. Ask what each required you to do. A research assignment may demonstrate data collection and analysis; an event role may demonstrate scheduling and coordination.

Avoid translating every activity into leadership. Sometimes the strongest evidence is careful execution, technical problem-solving or reliable support.

## Describe your personal contribution

For group work, separate the shared goal from your responsibility. Explain the part you designed, analysed or delivered and how it connected to the group output.

For example, “prepared the data-cleaning worksheet and summary charts for a group consumer-research project” is clearer than “completed a successful research project.” Add tools and methods only if you used them.

## Select evidence for the application

A university society position may be valuable for an operations internship if you coordinated people and deadlines. The same role might need less space on a technical CV where a software project provides stronger evidence.

Keep a private evidence bank so you can choose different examples without changing the facts. Tailoring should change emphasis, not create a different history.

## Present education and learning honestly

State the qualification, institution and expected completion. Include relevant modules or results when useful, but avoid overwhelming the CV with a transcript-like list.

Label certifications separately from informal learning. If a project is ongoing, describe completed work and current status rather than claiming a finished result.

## Make evidence accessible

Link to selected work when you have permission to share it. Add a short explanation so the reader understands the context and your role. Remove private data, group members' personal information and material your university or client has not authorised for publication.

Use a [CV review](/services/packages/cv-review) to check whether the evidence supports your target. [Student CV writing](/services/packages/cv-writing) can help organise a draft when your experience is spread across several activities.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can society membership go on my CV?

It can, but actual responsibilities or contributions are more informative than membership alone. Prioritise activities that support the application.

### Should I include an unfinished project?

Yes, when relevant and clearly labelled as ongoing. Describe what you have completed and avoid presenting expected outcomes as achieved results.
