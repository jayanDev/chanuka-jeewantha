# Why Tables, Icons and Text Boxes Can Damage an ATS-Friendly CV

Tables, icons and text boxes can create problems when they separate information that should be read together. Their presence alone does not prove a CV will fail.

The practical question is whether your finished document communicates the same career history visually and when its text is extracted. Review the specific layout rather than relying on an absolute rule about design elements.

## Understand what can become disconnected

A table may place a role title in one cell and its dates in another. A text box may sit beside a paragraph visually while appearing elsewhere in the document's reading order. An icon may stand in for a label that a reader needs in words.

These issues matter most when they affect contact details, employment history or qualifications. Decorative elements that carry no essential information are a different concern from elements that contain your only email address.

## Replace structure with simpler structure

Try writing each role as a short heading followed by employer, dates and bullets. Use normal paragraph spacing and alignment controls. Avoid using repeated spaces to imitate columns, because they can shift when fonts or viewers change.

For skills, replace a visual rating with a specific capability. “Prepared monthly Excel reconciliation worksheets” tells the reader what you did. Five coloured circles cannot explain the same experience.

## Test before deciding

Copy all text from the exported file into a plain document. Read it without referring to the original. Check whether you can still associate each achievement with its role and each qualification with its institution.

Greenhouse's guidance identifies tables, headers and text boxes among formatting concerns that can affect parsing. That is a reason for caution, not evidence that all systems behave identically. See [the vendor's guidance](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse).

## Preserve professional presentation

You can retain a polished appearance with consistent typography, spacing and restrained emphasis. If a visual layout is important for a creative application, consider linking to a portfolio while keeping the core career information straightforward.

Ask a second reader to review the simplified version. If it is easier to understand but looks too dense, adjust spacing and remove repetition before reintroducing complicated containers.

## Check the actual upload

An employer's requested format takes priority. Review any extracted application fields and correct errors where possible. Keep the file you submitted so you can identify a problem later.

For document-specific help, send the editable file and final export to a [CV review](/services/packages/cv-review). A screenshot alone cannot reveal the document's text structure.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Must every line or icon be removed?

No. Focus on whether essential information remains written, connected and readable. Remove decoration that creates confusion or consumes needed space.

### Is a table in Word always unsafe?

No universal answer applies. Ordinary paragraphs are often easier to maintain, and extraction checks can reveal problems in your particular file.
