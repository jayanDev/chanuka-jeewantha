# How to Tailor Your CV for Every Job Without Rewriting Everything

Tailoring a CV is faster when you maintain a complete master record and change only the emphasis needed for each vacancy. Your factual history should remain consistent.

The aim is not to produce a new professional identity for every application. It is to help each employer find the evidence most relevant to its role.

## Build a master evidence bank

Keep accurate titles, dates, responsibilities, projects and achievements in one private document. Include notes about results and where the information can be verified. This master can be longer than an application CV because it is a source record, not a submission.

Group achievements by themes such as customer service, analysis, coordination or leadership. That makes it easier to find relevant examples without searching through old application files.

## Identify the vacancy's main priorities

Read essential requirements and responsibilities. Select the few priorities that define the role, then match them to your strongest evidence. Do not treat every word in a long advertisement as equally important.

If your evidence is weak for an essential requirement, consider whether the application is suitable. Tailoring cannot turn a missing qualification into a completed one.

## Edit a small set of areas

- Adjust the summary to introduce the relevant career direction.
- Reorder the skills section around genuine role requirements.
- Select or reorder achievements within recent roles.
- Choose projects that demonstrate the required capabilities.
- Remove detail that distracts from the application.

Keep employers, official titles, dates and qualification status unchanged. Do not move dates or alter seniority to make the match look stronger.

## Use role-family versions carefully

If you regularly apply for closely related roles, maintain a small number of base versions. An operations-focused CV and a customer-success-focused CV might draw from the same history but prioritise different evidence.

Review each against the actual vacancy before sending. A base version is a starting point, not permission to skip reading the advertisement.

## Finish with a consistency check

Compare the CV with your cover letter and application answers. Search for another employer's name, stale role titles and old filenames. Open the export and save the exact submitted version with the vacancy.

This routine makes later interview preparation easier: you can revisit the examples the employer has already seen. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Must I rewrite every bullet for every job?

No. Keep strong relevant evidence and change its emphasis where needed. Many factual sections can remain identical across related applications.

### How many CV versions should I keep?

Keep as few base versions as you can manage reliably, plus records of actual submissions. Avoid a large collection of inconsistent drafts.
