# How to Write an ATS-Friendly CV That Recruiters Can Actually Find

Writing an ATS-friendly CV starts with a clear target role and evidence of relevant work. Useful terminology should help a recruiter understand your experience.

Do not treat the document as a collection of search terms. A CV that names the right tools but never explains how you used them may be easy to search and difficult to evaluate.

## Make a requirement-to-evidence worksheet

Read one representative vacancy and identify its main responsibilities, tools and qualifications. For each, write a real example from your experience. Separate direct evidence, transferable evidence and genuine gaps.

For a finance role, the worksheet might connect bank reconciliation to your monthly reconciliation work, Excel to a reporting workbook and audit support to preparing supporting schedules. Do not label a gap as experience simply because the keyword appears in the advert.

## Use recognisable, accurate terms

Write the normal name of a tool or skill where it belongs. If an acronym could be unclear, include the full term once. Avoid renaming genuine roles to make them appear more senior; a short functional explanation is safer when your internal title is unfamiliar.

For example, “Operations Associate — customer order coordination” can explain a broad title if the description is accurate. The official title remains visible for reference checks.

## Show the skill in action

Replace “SQL, SQL reporting, SQL analysis” with one specific example such as “used SQL queries to reconcile weekly order records and investigate missing transactions.” This illustrates the activity and purpose without claiming an unsupported outcome.

List important capabilities in the skills section, then support them in employment or project descriptions. Repetition is useful only when it adds context.

## Keep the structure predictable

Use clear headings and a consistent role format. Put contact details in readable text. Follow file-type instructions and inspect the final document after export.

When an application form imports information, check the populated fields. Correct a misplaced employer or missing qualification if the portal allows it. The file and the form should tell the same factual story.

## Review for a person as well as software

Ask whether the opening identifies your professional direction and whether the first relevant role shows concrete work. Remove words that add length without meaning. A reader should not have to decode internal abbreviations or infer your contribution from a list of tools.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should every keyword appear several times?

No fixed repetition count applies. Use a term where it helps identify or explain genuine experience, and remove unnecessary duplication.

### Can I include skills I am currently learning?

Yes, when relevant and clearly labelled as learning or project experience. Do not imply professional proficiency you have not developed.
