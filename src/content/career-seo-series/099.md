# LinkedIn Headline Examples: Stop Writing Only Your Job Title

A LinkedIn headline can explain more than your current title. Add relevant expertise or direction so a reader understands the professional context you want to communicate.

Keep every claim accurate. The examples below are illustrative structures to adapt, not credentials or experience you should copy without a factual basis.

## Use identity, strengths and context

Begin with your actual professional identity, then add a few useful capabilities. A specialist focus or industry context can help when it is relevant. Avoid crowding the headline with unrelated roles and vague adjectives.

A headline should be readable as a short introduction. It does not need to contain every skill from your CV or promise a business result.

## Examples for working professionals

- **Accountant | Financial Reporting, Reconciliations and Month-End Support** — suitable only when those activities reflect your experience.
- **Software Engineer | Backend Development, API Integration and Testing** — identifies a technical direction without an exhaustive stack list.
- **HR Executive | Recruitment Coordination and HR Operations** — clarifies the function rather than implying every HR specialism.
- **Operations Manager | Service Delivery, Team Development and Process Improvement** — requires actual management and relevant evidence.

Use a narrower version if some of the listed capabilities do not describe your work. Accuracy is more valuable than breadth.

## Examples for graduates and transitions

- **Business Analytics Graduate | SQL and Power BI Projects | Seeking Junior Analyst Opportunities** — distinguishes project evidence from professional analyst employment.
- **Marketing Undergraduate | Content Planning and Research Projects** — communicates current status and preparation.
- **Customer Service Professional | Moving Toward Service Operations | Reporting and Coordination** — makes a transition explicit.
- **Teacher | Training Design and Learner Assessment | Exploring Learning and Development Roles** — connects transferable work without inventing a corporate L&D title.

These examples are starting points. Adapt the wording to your target and keep it within the platform's current field limit.

## Remove weak headline material

Avoid unsupported labels such as top expert, guru or guaranteed results. Do not use a desired job title in a way that implies you currently hold it. Keep official employment details accurate in the experience section.

A headline can signal direction without concealing your background. Make sure the About section and experience provide evidence for its main claims.

## Test it with a reader

Ask someone what work they think you do and which roles the headline suggests. If the answer is unclear or too broad, simplify it. Compare the result with your CV's professional direction.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is my job title alone always wrong?

No. It can be sufficient, but relevant context may make an unfamiliar or broad title more informative.

### Can I put my target role in the headline?

Yes, if you make the intention clear and do not imply you already hold a role or qualification you have not obtained.
