# ATS Optimization vs Recruiter Optimization: Your CV Needs Both

A useful CV must be easy to process and worth reading. ATS optimisation and recruiter-focused writing address different parts of that requirement.

The first concerns accessible information and accurate terminology. The second concerns relevance, evidence and interpretation. Treat them as complementary checks rather than competing approaches.

## Start with a factual foundation

Gather correct dates, titles, qualifications and achievements. Identify the target role and match its requirements to your background. Without that foundation, formatting changes and keyword edits can make the document look stronger without making it more accurate.

Keep a separate record of supporting details so you can explain results and scope. Do not publish confidential evidence merely to make a claim appear more impressive.

## Perform a document-readability pass

Check section headings, contact details and the relationship between employers, roles and dates. Open the final file and inspect the reading order by copying text into a plain document. Follow upload instructions and check automatically populated fields.

This pass addresses usability. It does not establish that the candidate is qualified or that the document tells a compelling career story.

## Perform a recruiter-reading pass

Ask whether the opening identifies your professional direction. Check that relevant achievements are easy to locate and that the reader can distinguish your contribution from routine responsibilities.

An operations candidate might list inventory management as a skill. The recruiter-focused version also explains the actual work: reconciling stock records, investigating discrepancies and coordinating replenishment. The keyword becomes useful because it is connected to evidence.

## Resolve conflicts through clarity

If a keyword makes a sentence awkward, rewrite the sentence rather than repeating the word elsewhere. If a design hides important content, simplify the layout rather than creating a hidden text version.

Similarly, do not remove essential context just to make a document shorter. Edit repetition first, then decide which information the specific application needs.

## Use two separate review questions

The technical question is, “Can the information be read and associated correctly?” The editorial question is, “Does this information explain why I am relevant?” A strong CV needs sensible answers to both.

Ask another person to review the exported document without explanation. Their questions can reveal missing context, while an extraction check can reveal structural problems. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which should I optimise first?

Begin with accurate, relevant content. Then make the structure readable and review the final file from both technical and human perspectives.

### Can one simple CV satisfy both needs?

Yes. Clear headings, specific achievements, accurate terminology and consistent formatting can support software processing and human review together.
