# How to Match Your CV With a Job Description Without Keyword Stuffing

Matching a CV to a job description means making relevant evidence easier to find. It does not mean copying the advertisement into your experience section.

The employer describes what the role needs. Your CV should explain which of those needs your background prepares you to address, with a clear distinction between direct experience and transferable skills.

## Build a three-column match

Create columns for the requirement, your evidence and the best location in the CV. For “customer escalation handling,” your evidence might be resolving delayed-order complaints. For “team leadership,” it might be training colleagues rather than formally managing them.

That difference should remain visible. Do not convert informal training into a management title simply because the vacancy includes leadership responsibilities.

## Choose the strongest examples

If several achievements support one requirement, select the most relevant and recent example. Avoid crowding the CV with repetitive bullets. A focused example is easier to assess than several generic claims.

For instance, an account manager applying to a service-led role might prioritise renewals and complaint resolution. For a sales-led role, the same person might emphasise pipeline development and verified revenue contribution. Their dates and core responsibilities do not change.

## Edit the opening after the evidence

Once you know what supports the vacancy, revise the summary and skills section. The profile should introduce the experience already demonstrated below it. Writing it last can prevent an ambitious opening that the rest of the CV cannot support.

Use the employer's terminology where it accurately describes your work. If you have used a different system, name the real system and explain the relevant activity instead of silently replacing its name.

## Remove empty repetition

Search the draft for repeated phrases. Ask whether each occurrence adds new information. A skill can appear in both the skills section and an experience example, but repeating it in every bullet usually reduces readability.

Never use white text or a pasted job description hidden at the end of the file. The document should be honest and useful to anyone who reads or extracts it.

## Perform a final application check

Compare the CV against the essential requirements one more time. Check the cover letter, application answers and filename for references to another employer. Save the tailored version with enough detail to identify it later.

If you need help deciding which evidence to prioritise, bring the vacancy and your master CV to a [professional review](/services/packages/cv-review). The quality of the match depends on the facts available, not on reaching an arbitrary word count or score.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I use the same employment history for several roles?

Yes. Keep the facts consistent while changing which relevant achievements you emphasise and how you introduce your experience.

### What if I do not meet one requirement?

Identify whether it is essential or preferred. Explain genuine transferable experience where appropriate, and do not claim a credential or skill you lack.
