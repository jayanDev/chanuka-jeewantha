# Your CV Looks Beautiful — But Can an ATS Actually Read It?

A polished CV should remain understandable when its visual decoration is removed. Testing the exported file helps you find problems that are easy to miss on screen.

You do not need access to an employer's system to perform basic checks. You do need to recognise their limits: a successful text copy is useful evidence of readability, not certification for every ATS.

## Inspect the document in two ways

First, read the CV visually. Check whether headings, role dates and important achievements are easy to locate. Then copy its text into a plain document and read it from the beginning.

Does the first employer still connect to the correct title? Do dates appear beside the right roles? Has a sidebar interrupted the middle of a sentence? Record the actual problem instead of assuming that every attractive layout is unsafe.

## Watch for information carried only by graphics

An email icon does not replace the email address. A row of stars does not explain proficiency in Excel. A company logo does not identify an employer as clearly as its written name.

Replace visual skill ratings with meaningful descriptions. “Excel: pivot tables, reconciliation worksheets and lookup formulas” gives a reader more usable information than “Excel: 90%.” Include only skills you can demonstrate.

## Keep important relationships intact

Try putting employer, role and dates in a consistent sequence. Use ordinary text for the information that determines your professional history. If you retain a sidebar, ensure that it does not carry the only copy of essential details or disrupt the exported reading order.

Greenhouse lists complex formatting among possible parsing problems, which is a reason to test your document rather than judge it only by appearance. See [its troubleshooting notes](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse).

## Recheck after every export

The file you edit and the file you upload may behave differently. Open the final PDF or Word document, check every page and test its links. Look for split bullets, missing symbols and text that has become too small.

Ask someone unfamiliar with your career to identify your target role and strongest relevant achievement. If they cannot, the issue may be information hierarchy rather than technical extraction.

## Keep design working for you

Use spacing, bold headings and a restrained accent colour to guide attention. Keep a separate portfolio for visual work when useful. The CV can link to that portfolio while staying easy to read and update.

If your document needs a second pair of eyes, request a [CV review](/services/packages/cv-review) and include the actual exported file, not just a screenshot of the design.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is every two-column CV unreadable?

No. Results depend on the document and processing system. Test the reading order and simplify layouts that scramble information.

### Should I submit a screenshot of my CV?

Use the employer's requested format. A screenshot can make text extraction and editing harder than a properly exported text-based document.
