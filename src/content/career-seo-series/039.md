# Should Your CV Include Your NIC, Age, Religion or Marital Status?

Include personal information in a CV only when it has a clear application purpose. Do not copy sensitive fields from an old template without questioning them.

This is a practical privacy and document-design approach, not legal advice about every employer or country. Formal application forms may have separate requirements that should be assessed in their own context.

## Start with information needed for contact

Your name, professional email, phone number and relevant location are usually the useful starting details. Add a LinkedIn or portfolio link when it supports the application.

A full home address often provides more detail than the reader needs at the first stage. City or region can communicate location without distributing your precise residential address widely.

## Treat identity numbers cautiously

An NIC or passport number is different from a contact detail. Ask why it is needed and whether a verified, secure application process requests it separately. Do not place identity-document scans inside a publicly shared CV.

Check the employer's authenticity before supplying sensitive information. A request arriving through an unverified message deserves investigation even if it uses a familiar company name.

## Question unrelated personal categories

Age, religion and marital status generally do not explain professional capability. Consider whether they contribute anything necessary to evaluating the role. A template field is not evidence that the employer requires it.

For UK applications, the National Careers Service advises excluding age, date of birth, marital status and nationality from the CV. See its [official CV guidance](https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/careers-advice/cv-sections). Keep country-specific decisions separate from assumptions about every market.

## Protect other people's information too

Referee phone numbers and email addresses belong to other people. Get permission before sharing them and consider supplying them at the stage the employer requests references.

Do not include client names, patient information or confidential internal records as proof of your work. Describe achievements in a way that respects the information you are allowed to disclose.

## Maintain a shareable version

Keep a CV suitable for sending to recruiters or uploading to professional platforms. Provide additional verified information through the appropriate channel only when necessary. Review old copies so sensitive fields do not reappear during tailoring.

If you request a [CV review](/services/packages/cv-review), ask the reviewer to check unnecessary personal information as well as wording and layout. A professional document should communicate value without distributing irrelevant private details.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What if an official form asks for information omitted from my CV?

Assess the genuine form and its purpose separately. Omitting a field from a CV does not mean ignoring a legitimate application requirement.

### Should I send my NIC with a CV writing enquiry?

An initial career-writing discussion should not normally need an identity-document copy. Ask why any sensitive document is requested before sharing it.
