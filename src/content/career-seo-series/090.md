# Generic vs Tailored Cover Letters: Why One Gets Ignored

A generic cover letter makes the reader work out your relevance. A tailored letter explains the connection between your evidence and the specific opportunity.

No wording guarantees attention, and you cannot know why every letter receives no response. Still, tailoring can make the application more informative and reduce avoidable mismatches.

## Recognise generic writing

Generic letters praise a reputed company, promise hard work and repeat broad skills. The employer name can often be changed without altering the rest of the text.

That flexibility is convenient, but it can leave the reader with little evidence of fit or genuine interest. A template should provide structure, not all the substance.

## Identify the vacancy's priorities

Select a few responsibilities that define the role. Match them to real examples from your background, then choose the strongest ones for the letter.

For an operations role, coordination and accurate reporting may matter. For a client-facing role, service recovery and relationship work may deserve more emphasis. The same candidate may have evidence for both, but should not present it identically.

## Explain the connection explicitly

Instead of “I have excellent organisational skills,” describe a relevant task and why it prepares you for the work. Keep the example concise and accurate.

An illustrative example might explain maintaining a shared request tracker and coordinating follow-up with two teams. Do not add an unsupported result merely to make the paragraph seem stronger.

## Add a genuine reason for interest

Research the role and organisation through reliable information. Explain a specific connection to your direction or experience. Avoid invented referrals, unsupported claims about the company or generic admiration.

The motivation paragraph should belong to this application. If it fits any employer equally well, make it more specific or shorter.

## Use a reusable process

Keep an evidence bank and a basic paragraph structure. For each vacancy, update the priorities, examples and motivation, then check all names and attachments.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is changing the company name enough?

No. The letter should also connect your evidence and motivation to the role's actual requirements.

### Must every sentence be new?

No. Accurate relevant examples can be reused. Adapt the selection and explanation rather than rewriting good material merely for novelty.
