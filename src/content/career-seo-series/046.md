# No Experience? These 8 Things Can Still Make Your CV Strong

Paid employment is only one source of CV evidence. These eight areas can help a beginner show relevant preparation, provided each is described accurately.

Choose the examples that support your target role. You do not need all eight, and collecting headings is less useful than explaining a few meaningful contributions.

## Academic and practical evidence

1. **Relevant education.** State your qualification and study status, with selected subjects or results when they help assess the role.
2. **Projects.** Explain the problem, your contribution, tools and output rather than listing only a title.
3. **Research.** Describe how you collected, checked or analysed information and communicated the findings.
4. **Practical training.** Include relevant courses or workshops, distinguishing completion from ongoing study.

For example, a spreadsheet project can show careful data handling if you explain the task and checks performed. A course title alone does not prove the same level of application.

## Evidence from responsibility

5. **Volunteering.** Describe actual tasks, such as scheduling activities or maintaining records.
6. **Student leadership.** Explain what you coordinated and your decision-making responsibility, not just the title.
7. **Part-time or family-business work.** Show relevant service, administration or operational tasks in their real context.
8. **A personal portfolio project.** Demonstrate a useful capability through work you can explain and are permitted to share.

Be careful with ownership. If a group built an application, identify the part you developed. If you helped at a family business, do not invent a formal management position.

## Turn an activity into a CV entry

Use a clear label, organisation or context, dates and a concise explanation of your contribution. An illustrative entry might describe coordinating volunteer attendance and maintaining an event checklist.

Add a result only when supported. Completing a useful deliverable is evidence; it does not require an invented percentage improvement.

## Avoid filling every gap with adjectives

Generic claims such as hardworking, passionate and reliable need context. Let your examples demonstrate those qualities. Keep a shorter CV if that is what the evidence supports.

Review the vacancy again and select the strongest examples for it. The [free career resources](/resources) can help you begin, while [student CV writing support](/services/packages/cv-writing) can help organise a scattered set of activities into a focused application.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are personal projects less valuable than university projects?

Not automatically. A relevant project with a clear contribution and accessible evidence can be useful regardless of where it began.

### Should I include every activity from school?

No. Select relevant evidence and reduce older or unrelated detail as stronger experience becomes available.
