# How to Write an Internship CV That Does Not Look Like Every Other Student CV

An internship CV becomes distinctive through specific evidence, not unusual colours or a dramatic personal statement. Show the work only you can truthfully describe.

Many student CVs use the same objectives and skill lists. Replace those shared phrases with clear examples of preparation for the particular placement.

## Lead with the placement fit

Identify the internship's main tasks and choose two or three relevant strengths. A short opening can introduce your study area, practical preparation and intended contribution.

Avoid promising to transform the business as an intern. Show readiness to support real work, learn responsibly and complete tasks at the expected level.

## Give one project enough explanation

Instead of listing six unexplained projects, describe a few relevant ones well. Explain the problem, your role, tools and output. Include a limitation when it helps distinguish a prototype from a finished product.

An illustrative marketing example might describe developing a content calendar for a university initiative and reviewing engagement patterns. Do not invent commercial campaign results for work that never involved a paying client.

## Show evidence of reliability

Use an example of meeting a deadline, checking data, coordinating a group or responding to feedback. Explain the behaviour rather than simply listing “time management” and “teamwork.”

If you held a society role, distinguish the title from the contribution. Organising the registration process is more informative than naming a committee position without context.

## Make a small portfolio useful

Where relevant, share a few selected outputs with short descriptions. Check that the links open and that you have permission to publish the material. Remove personal information and private group files.

A repository or design gallery is stronger when the reader can understand what you contributed. Do not assume screenshots alone explain the project.

## Edit the generic material out

Remove copied objectives, unsupported proficiency ratings and repetitive subject lists. Keep contact details, study status and requested availability clear. Use a simple layout that gives evidence room to breathe.

Before submission, ask a reviewer what they learned about you that would not apply to every classmate. If the answer is little, add more specific evidence. Explore [student CV writing](/services/packages/cv-writing) or a [CV review](/services/packages/cv-review) for help with that editing process.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a creative design to stand out?

No. Relevant, specific examples and clear writing can distinguish your application within a simple professional layout.

### Can I reuse a classmate's wording?

Use examples to understand structure, but write about your own contribution. Shared wording can introduce claims that do not describe your experience.
